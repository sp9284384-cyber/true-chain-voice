     1→Introduction — Anonymous Reporting of Harassment or Corruption
     2→
     3→Omni_CyberTech_15: Anonymous Reporting of Harassment or Corruption
     4→focuses on building a secure digital platform that allows individuals to
     5→report workplace harassment, corruption, misconduct, abuse of authority,
     6→or other sensitive incidents anonymously. The official problem statement
     7→highlights that many such incidents remain unreported because people
     8→fear retaliation or other consequences, creating a need for a secure and
     9→anonymous reporting mechanism.
    10→
    11→In many organizations and institutions, victims or witnesses may
    12→hesitate to report wrongdoing because they do not trust existing
    13→complaint systems. Traditional reporting mechanisms often require
    14→personal details such as a name, email address, phone number, employee
    15→ID, or other identifying information. Even when anonymity is promised,
    16→users may still worry that their identity could be discovered through
    17→uploaded documents, images, metadata, IP information, or internal
    18→records. This lack of trust can discourage people from reporting genuine
    19→incidents.
    20→
    21→The proposed solution is therefore a privacy-preserving anonymous
    22→reporting platform designed around the principle of "report without
    23→revealing your identity." The platform would allow a user to submit a
    24→complaint, description, and supporting evidence without unnecessarily
    25→collecting personally identifiable information. At the same time, the
    26→system should provide authorized investigators with enough information
    27→to investigate the incident while protecting the reporter's identity.
    28→
    29→A major challenge is ensuring that anonymity does not come at the cost
    30→of evidence integrity. Digital evidence can potentially be modified,
    31→deleted, or manipulated after submission. Therefore, the proposed system
    32→can use cryptographic hashing and a hash-chained tamper-evident audit
    33→log to record the integrity of reports and evidence. Each record can be
    34→cryptographically linked to the previous record, making unauthorized
    35→modifications detectable.
    36→
    37→Another important aspect is privacy-preserving evidence handling.
    38→Uploaded images, documents, or videos may contain hidden metadata such
    39→as device information, timestamps, GPS coordinates, or software details.
    40→Before storing evidence, the platform can sanitize unnecessary metadata
    41→and encrypt sensitive files. This reduces the possibility of
    42→accidentally exposing information that could identify the reporter.
    43→
    44→The system can further incorporate AI-assisted analysis to help
    45→authorized investigators prioritize and organize reports. For example,
    46→submitted reports could be classified by category, urgency, or potential
    47→risk. Similar reports could also be clustered while applying
    48→privacy-preserving techniques to reduce the possibility of identifying
    49→individual reporters.

   111→Key Objectives
   112→
   113→1.  Protect reporter anonymity by minimizing collection of personally
   114→    identifiable information.
   115→
   116→2.  Secure sensitive reports and evidence through encryption and
   117→    controlled access.
   118→
   119→3.  Remove unnecessary metadata from uploaded files to reduce identity
   120→    leakage.
   121→
   122→4.  Ensure evidence integrity using cryptographic hashing and
   123→    tamper-evident logs.
   124→
   125→5.  Detect unauthorized modifications to submitted reports.
   126→
   127→6.  Assist investigators through AI-based classification,
   128→    prioritization, and report clustering.
   129→
   130→7.  Build digital trust so users can confidently report sensitive
   131→    incidents without fear of retaliation.
   132→
   133→Core Innovation
   134→
   135→The project is not simply an anonymous complaint website. Its core
   136→innovation is combining:
   137→
   138→Anonymous Reporting + Privacy Protection + Applied Cryptography +
   139→Evidence Integrity + AI Assistance
   140→
   141→This makes the platform a privacy-preserving digital trust system for
   142→sensitive reporting.

   353→Why it's structured this way:
   354→
   355→-   reports never stores a name/email/employee-ID — the session_token is
   356→    the only thing linking a submission back to the person who filed it,
   357→    and only they know it
   358→
   359→-   prev_hash on both reports and status_updates is what makes the chain
   360→    — each new row cryptographically depends on the one before it, so
   361→    editing history breaks verification
   362→
   363→-   status_updates is separate from reports.status being directly
   364→    editable — this is deliberate: investigators never mutate a report
   365→    row, they append a new linked update, which is what lets you
   366→    honestly say "nothing here is ever overwritten"
   367→
   368→-   embedding sits on the report row itself so clustering doesn't need a
   369→    separate service call every time

   416→What each service does:
   417→
   418→crypto.py — two jobs: (1) encrypts report content before it touches the
   419→DB, decrypts it only when an authenticated investigator pulls it up; (2)
   420→computes SHA256(content + evidence_hash + prev_hash) for chain-linking.
   421→Keep these separate functions so encryption and hashing are
   422→independently testable.
   423→
   424→sanitize.py — runs on every file upload before storage.
   425→Uses Pillow's Image.info clearing for images, PyPDF2/pikepdf metadata
   426→stripping for PDFs. Returns both the cleaned file and a hash of it,
   427→which feeds into the report's chain hash.
   428→
   429→ai_triage.py — calls your local Ollama instance with a structured
   430→prompt: "Classify this report into one of [categories] and assign
   431→urgency [low/med/high]. Return JSON only." Also generates a
   432→sentence-embedding vector (via sentence-transformers) stored on the
   433→report for later clustering — no training, purely inference calls.
   434→
   435→chain_verify.py — the demo centerpiece. Walks every report in order,
   436→recomputes each hash from stored content, and compares it against the
   437→stored report_hash. Returns the first row where they diverge, if any.
   438→This is what you call live when you "break" a record on stage.

   470→Flow through the system, end to end:
   471→
   472→1.  Report submitted → crypto.py encrypts content → sanitize.py cleans
   473→    any attached file → ai_triage.py classifies + embeds
   474→    → crypto.py computes the chain hash using the previous report's hash
   475→    → row written to reports
   476→
   477→2.  Investigator opens dashboard → backend decrypts content on the fly
   478→    for authorized viewing only → sorted by AI-assigned urgency
   479→
   480→3.  Status change → new row in status_updates, chained the same way,
   481→    original reports row untouched
   482→
   483→4.  Verification → chain_verify.py recomputes every hash in sequence and
   484→    flags the first mismatch
   485→
   486→This gives you a clean separation: anonymity logic lives in
   487→how reports is structured (no PII columns, ever), integrity logic lives
   488→entirely in the hash-chain service, and AI is a thin inference layer
   489→that doesn't touch either of those concerns. That separation is also a
   490→good thing to say out loud to judges — it shows the privacy and
   491→integrity guarantees don't depend on the AI working correctly.

   604→Why each piece exists
   605→
   606→app/report/ — the entire product exists to make this page trustworthy.
   607→It's deliberately isolated from anything auth-related in the folder
   608→tree, so there's no accidental code path where a submission could get
   609→tied to a logged-in session.
   610→
   611→app/report/confirmation/ — separate page (not a modal) so the
   612→session_token is the only thing shown, with nothing else on screen that
   613→could be screenshotted alongside identifying context. Also gives you a
   614→clean demo beat: "here's the token, and that's the only thing that
   615→connects you to this report."
   616→
   617→app/status/[token]/ — lets a reporter check on their submission without
   618→ever creating an account. Dynamic route on the token itself keeps this
   619→stateless from your side — you're not maintaining reporter sessions
   620→anywhere.
   621→
   622→app/verify/ — a public page, separate from the investigator dashboard,
   623→where anyone (including judges) can trigger the chain-verification check
   624→and watch it work. Keeping this outside /investigator/ means you can
   625→demo integrity without needing to log in on stage, which makes your live
   626→demo faster and less failure-prone.
   627→
   628→app/investigator/login/ + /dashboard/ — the only part of the app that
   629→needs real auth. Isolating it under one route lets middleware.ts protect
   630→the whole subtree with one rule instead of guarding pages individually.
   631→
   632→app/investigator/dashboard/[id]/ — dedicated detail view per report,
   633→because this is where decrypted content gets shown — you want that
   634→rendering logic in exactly one place, not scattered across a shared card
   635→component that might also render for unauthenticated contexts.
   636→
   637→components/report/FileUpload.tsx — its own component (not folded into
   638→ReportForm.tsx) because it needs to show the "metadata stripped ✓"
   639→confirmation after the backend sanitizes the file — that's a distinct
   640→piece of state and UI feedback worth isolating and testing on its own.
   641→
   642→components/investigator/IntegrityBadge.tsx — separate from
   643→UrgencyBadge.tsx even though both are small badges, because integrity
   644→status (verified/tampered) and urgency (low/med/high) come from
   645→completely different backend services (hash-chain vs. AI triage) —
   646→keeping them as separate components means a bug in one never touches the
   647→other's rendering logic.
   648→
   649→components/investigator/StatusUpdateForm.tsx — deliberately doesn't let
   650→an investigator edit the original report text, only append a new status.
   651→The component's constrained API (it can only call the PATCH /status
   652→endpoint, never PUT /reports) enforces your "nothing is ever
   653→overwritten" guarantee at the UI layer too, not just the backend.
   654→
   655→components/verify/ChainVisualizer.tsx — a visual representation of the
   656→hash chain (boxes linked by arrows, with a red break shown when
   657→tampering is detected) — this is worth building as its own component
   658→because it's your single strongest visual for judges; don't bury it
   659→inline in a page file where it's harder to polish.
   660→
   661→components/shared/PrivacyNotice.tsx — reusable banner ("We never collect
   662→your name, email, or IP") shown on both the report form and confirmation
   663→page — a shared component ensures the privacy promise is worded
   664→identically everywhere, which matters when judges are evaluating trust.
   665→
   666→lib/api.ts — every fetch call to your FastAPI backend goes through here
   667→instead of being scattered in components. This makes it trivial to point
   668→to a different backend URL for demo vs. local dev, and keeps components
   669→free of fetch/error-handling boilerplate.
   670→
   671→lib/auth.ts — isolates investigator token storage/refresh logic so it's
   672→the only file in the frontend that ever touches anything
   673→identity-related — easy to point to if someone asks "where does auth
   674→live in your codebase."
   675→
   676→lib/types.ts — shared Report, Evidence, StatusUpdate interfaces matching
   677→your backend Pydantic schemas, so the frontend and backend contract
   678→stays in sync as you iterate under time pressure.
   679→
   680→middleware.ts — Next.js middleware that checks for a valid investigator
   681→session before allowing any /investigator/* route to render, redirecting
   682→to /investigator/login otherwise. One file, one place to reason about
   683→who can see decrypted report content.

   685→Connection Map
   686→
   687→1. Report Submission Flow
   688→
   689→Frontend: app/report/page.tsx → components/report/ReportForm.tsx
   690→Calls: lib/api.ts → submitReport()
   691→
   692→POST /reports
   693→
   694→  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
   695→  Layer      What happens
   696→  ---------- -------------------------------------------------------------
   697→  Frontend   ReportForm.tsx collects description + category, calls
   698→             submitReport(data)
   699→
   700→  Backend    routers/reports.py receives payload
   701→  route      
   702→
   703→  Service    crypto.py → encrypt_content() encrypts the text
   704→
   705→  Service    crypto.py → hash_chain_link() computes report_hash using the
   706→             last row's hash
   707→
   708→  Database   INSERT INTO reports (session_token, encrypted_content,
   709→             report_hash, prev_hash, ...)
   710→
   711→  Response   { session_token: "abc123", report_id: 42 }
   712→
   713→  Frontend   Redirects to app/report/confirmation/page.tsx, displays
   714→             session_token only
   715→  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
   716→
   717→2. Evidence Upload
   718→
   719→Frontend: components/report/FileUpload.tsx (nested inside
   720→ReportForm.tsx)
   721→Calls: lib/api.ts → uploadEvidence()
   722→
   723→POST /reports/{id}/evidence
   724→
   725→  -------------------------------------------------------------------------
   726→  Layer      What happens
   727→  ---------- --------------------------------------------------------------
   728→  Frontend   User attaches file, sent as multipart/form-data with report_id
   729→
   730→  Backend    routers/evidence.py receives file
   731→  route      
   732→
   733→  Service    sanitize.py → strip_exif() / strip_pdf_metadata() cleans the
   734→             file
   735→
   736→  Service    crypto.py encrypts the sanitized file, computes file_hash
   737→
   738→  Service    Re-runs hash_chain_link() on the parent report since
   739→             evidence_hash feeds into report_hash
   740→
   741→  Database   INSERT INTO evidence (report_id, sanitized_file_path,
   742→             file_hash, original_metadata_removed)
   743→
   744→  Database   UPDATE — actually avoid UPDATE here; instead recompute and
   745→             store report_hash only if evidence is attached at submission
   746→             time (design choice: require evidence upload before final
   747→             report hash is committed, not after)
   748→
   749→  Response   { evidence_id: 7, metadata_removed: ["GPS", "device_model",
   750→             "timestamp"] }
   751→
   752→  Frontend   FileUpload.tsx shows "Metadata removed: GPS, device model,
   753→             timestamp ✓"

   781→4. AI Triage (happens server-side, not directly frontend-triggered)
   782→
   783→Triggered by: the POST /reports flow, automatically, right after
   784→encryption
   785→
   786→Internal call — not a frontend-facing endpoint
   787→
   788→  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
   789→  Layer      What happens
   790→  ---------- -------------------------------------------------------------
   791→  Backend    routers/reports.py calls services/ai_triage.py right after

   793→             in-memory only)
   794→
   795→  Service    ai_triage.py → classify_report() sends prompt to local
   796→             Ollama, gets back category + urgency JSON
   797→
   798→  Service    ai_triage.py → generate_embedding() creates vector via
   799→             sentence-transformers
   800→
   801→  Database   Both written into the same reports row: category, urgency,
   802→             embedding

   908→9. Chain Verification (your demo centerpiece)
   909→
   910→Frontend: app/verify/page.tsx → components/verify/ChainVerifyPanel.tsx /

   912→Calls: lib/api.ts → verifyChain(id?)
   913→

   915→
   916→  -------------------------------------------------------------------------
   917→  Layer      What happens
   918→  ---------- --------------------------------------------------------------
   919→  Frontend   User clicks "Verify Integrity" — no auth required, this page
   920→             is public
   921→
   922→  Backend    routers/verify.py triggers chain_verify.py
   923→  route      
   924→
   925→  Service    chain_verify.py walks every report in order, recomputes
   926→             SHA256(content + evidence_hash + prev_hash), compares to
   927→             stored report_hash
   928→
   929→  Database   SELECT id, report_hash, prev_hash, encrypted_content FROM
   930→             reports ORDER BY id (read-only, no writes)
   931→
   932→  Response   { valid: true } or { valid: false, broken_at: 3,
   933→             expected_hash: "...", found_hash: "..." }
   934→
   935→  Frontend   ChainVisualizer.tsx renders the chain as linked boxes; the
   936→             broken link turns red if valid: false
